The tape-out checklist: what to confirm before you send GDS

Physical design & tape-out Intermediate 27 Nov 2026 5 min read By Ketuna

Once GDS leaves your hands, nothing can be changed. Work through a tape-out checklist covering physical checks, timing at every corner, precheck, documentation and a bring-up plan, with a small script to gate a run.

Tape-out is the one step in chip design that cannot be undone. Software can be patched after release; a mask set cannot. The cost of a mistake found after tape-out is a respin, months of waiting and the price of another shuttle slot or mask set. That is why teams use a written checklist, filled in with evidence, and review it with someone who did not do the work. This article lays out such a checklist for a digital block or small chip, and explains why each line is there.

Rule one: evidence, not feelings

Every item on the list should point to a file: a report, a log line, a screenshot, a commit hash. "DRC is clean" is a claim. "DRC is clean: magic_drc.rpt from run final-03, zero violations, commit a1b2c3d" is evidence that someone else can check. Flows print reassuring messages such as "Flow complete"; those are not sign-off. The checklist exists to force the step from claim to evidence.

It also forces you to sign off one specific run. If you change anything, even one constraint, the evidence from the previous run no longer applies.

Physical verification

Check What "done" means Why
DRC Zero violations with the foundry or shuttle sign-off deck on the final GDS The router's own check is not the sign-off deck
LVS Layout matches the final netlist, including power and physical cells Proves the polygons are the circuit you verified
Antenna Zero violations after diode insertion Plasma damage during fabrication can kill gates
Density Every layer within its density window, fill inserted Polishing needs even metal; fill changes parasitics
GDS integrity The GDS opens, top cell name is correct, no empty or unexpected cells A wrong top cell or missing sub-cell is a silent disaster

Run DRC and LVS on the final GDS, after fill and after any last edits, not on an intermediate file.

Timing and electrical

  • Setup and hold met at every sign-off corner, with extracted parasitics, not estimates. Record the worst slack per corner, not just the overall result.
  • Transition, capacitance and fanout limits met. Max slew and max capacitance violations are often ignored because they are not "timing", but they mean cells are driven outside their characterised range.
  • Constraints reviewed. No unexplained false paths, no unconstrained endpoints, clocks defined with the right periods.
  • IR drop and electromigration within limits for your power grid.
  • Equivalence checked between the RTL you verified and the final netlist, or at least gate-level simulation of the final netlist passing your key tests.

A small script to gate a run

Open flows write their results to a metrics file, so part of the checklist can be automated. This Python script reads a LibreLane metrics.json and checks it against sign-off rules. The metric names are the ones LibreLane writes; check them against your version.

import json
import sys

# (metric key, rule, limit, meaning)
RULES = [
    ("timing__setup__ws", ">=", 0.0, "setup slack, worst corner"),
    ("timing__hold__ws", ">=", 0.0, "hold slack, worst corner"),
    ("route__drc_errors", "==", 0, "DRC after detailed route"),
    ("design__lvs_error__count", "==", 0, "LVS errors"),
    ("route__antenna_violation__count", "==", 0, "antenna"),
    ("design__max_slew_violation__count", "==", 0, "max slew"),
    ("design__max_cap_violation__count", "==", 0, "max capacitance"),
]

def gate(path):
    with open(path) as f:
        m = json.load(f)
    ok = True
    for key, rule, limit, meaning in RULES:
        if key not in m:
            print(f"MISSING  {meaning:28s} ({key})")
            ok = False
            continue
        v = m[key]
        good = v >= limit if rule == ">=" else v == limit
        ok &= good
        print(f"{'PASS' if good else 'FAIL':8s} {meaning:28s} {v}")
    return ok

if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(0 if gate(sys.argv[1]) else 1)

Note that a missing metric counts as a failure. A gate that passes when the number it needs is absent is worse than no gate at all. A script like this covers the numeric items; it cannot judge whether your constraints are right or your documentation is clear.

Submission and precheck

Shuttle programmes run a precheck on submissions: an automated set of checks for the rules of that shuttle, such as the die size and position, the names and positions of the top-level pins, the absence of forbidden layers, and a clean DRC with their deck. Run the precheck yourself, well before the deadline. A design that passes your flow but fails precheck because a pin is on the wrong layer is a common last-day crisis.

Also confirm:

  • the exact files the shuttle requires (GDS, netlists, LEF, documentation, metadata) are all present and from the same run,
  • the commit of your design repository and the versions of the PDK and flow are recorded,
  • the submission was made from a clean checkout, not from a working directory with local edits.

Documentation and bring-up plan

The chip will come back months later, possibly to someone else's desk. Before tape-out, write:

  • A pinout table: every pin, its direction, function, voltage and reset state.
  • A register or command map if the design has one.
  • Expected idle and active current, at least as an estimate, so bring-up can tell normal from abnormal.
  • A bring-up plan: power-up sequence, the first things to check, the simplest test that proves the chip is alive, and the test vectors from simulation that the bench setup will replay.
  • Known limitations and any waived checks, each with the reason and who approved it.

Writing the bring-up plan before tape-out often exposes design problems: a missing way to observe internal state, a reset that cannot be applied, a test mode that cannot be entered from the pins. Those can still be fixed.

Review it with someone else

Go through the list with a reviewer who did not build the design. Their job is to ask "show me" for every line. The questions that feel annoying at this stage are exactly the ones silicon will ask later.

Try it yourself

  1. Harden a small design with LibreLane on sky130 and run the script above on runs/<tag>/final/metrics.json.
  2. Deliberately tighten CLOCK_PERIOD until setup fails, rerun, and confirm the gate fails with a non-zero exit code.
  3. Run Magic DRC and Netgen LVS yourself on the final GDS and compare with the flow's results.
  4. Write the pinout table and a one-page bring-up plan for the design, then ask a friend to review both.
