Partners

Partnering for STEM skills

We work with colleges, companies and STEM programs to build practical skills in embedded systems, VLSI, systems engineering and product development, on open-source tools that cost nothing to license and that students can keep.

Start a conversation How a partnership works

Colleges and universities

Bring industry practice into the semester without new licence costs.

Faculty development

Hands-on programs for faculty on open-source RTL design, verification, physical design and embedded flows, using the same labs and checks our students use, so faculty can run them independently afterwards.

Semester-integrated courses

Lab-based modules mapped to existing courses in digital design, computer architecture, VLSI and embedded systems, with auto-checked labs, study guides and quizzes.

Lab setup on open-source tools

A pinned, reproducible lab image for student laptops or lab PCs, an environment checker, and a regression that proves every lab runs. No licence servers, no per-seat fees.

Student workshops

The free "Tape out in a weekend" workshop on campus, and short bootcamps on the common core or a track. About the workshop.

Industry

Help shape engineers you would want to hire, and grow the ones you have.

Hiring partners

Meet graduates at demo day and review public portfolios with verified projects, written specs, review records and AI logs, so you can see how a candidate works, not just what they claim.

Sponsored capstone problems

Propose a small, real problem for a mixed-track team of four to build over weeks 9 to 12: RTL block, verification, FPGA integration and firmware. You see the work as it happens and evaluate it at the end.

Upskilling Planned

Two- to five-day courses for working engineers: AI-assisted verification, open-source RTL-to-GDS flows, firmware testing and CI, systems engineering for hardware teams.

Guest reviews

Join a design review or demo day as a guest reviewer. Students get feedback from a fresh, experienced eye; your engineers get to know the cohort.

Schools and STEM programs Planned

Introductory workshops for school students and STEM clubs: what electronics is, how a chip is made from sand to silicon, and a first taste of logic design with free tools in a browser or on a laptop.

These are planned and not running yet. If you run a school or a STEM program and want to be among the first, tell us through the form below.

Ideas we are shaping

  • Logic gates to a working counter in an afternoon
  • A simple sensor project, from idea to working prototype

The wider ecosystem

Ketuna aims to align its programs with national skilling frameworks and with public chip-design and semiconductor skilling programs, so that what students learn here counts for more. We will describe any such alignment only once it is real.

Ketuna has no partnerships, endorsements, accreditations or certifications to announce today. This page will list partners only with their written permission.

What partners get

No licence cost

Every tool is open source. Students and faculty keep using them after the program.

Material that is ready

Labs with automated checks, 100 study guides, a quiz bank, slide decks and rubrics, maintained in one place.

Evidence, not claims

Public portfolios, CI results, review records and AI logs that show what was actually learned.

Industry practice

Mentors who are working engineers, and a professional layer of specs, reviews and status reporting every week.

Shaped to your needs

We start from the skills you want to build and fit the format, length and depth to them.

Honest reporting

Clear goals agreed up front, and a written review of outcomes at the end of each pilot.

How a partnership works

Start small, measure, then grow. Every partnership begins with a pilot that is easy to evaluate.
