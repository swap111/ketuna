What you will do

You work in pairs, starting from an electronic-dice design and ending with your own design and its layout. Two facilitators run the room, with one helper for every ten participants.

  1. Write Verilog

    Read and change a small synchronous design that fits the Tiny Tapeout tile.

  2. Test it

    Write cocotb tests in Python, lint with Verilator, synthesize with Yosys.

  3. Harden it

    Push to GitHub and run the Tiny Tapeout flow: synthesis, place and route, DRC and LVS.

  4. See your chip

    Open your layout in a GDS viewer and take a screenshot of your own silicon.

By Sunday evening you can

  • Explain the path from Verilog to GDS
  • Write and test a small synchronous design
  • Read a CI flow's reports and fix what fails
  • Use an AI assistant to write a variant, and find its bugs with tests

You leave with

  • A public repository with your design, tests and a green GDS run
  • A layout screenshot of your design
  • An AI log showing how you checked AI-written hardware
  • A working open-source toolchain on your laptop

About real silicon. Your design will be ready to submit to a Tiny Tapeout shuttle. Submitting is optional, done after the workshop, and the tile is paid for by you at the shuttle's current price and deadline. Shuttle schedules and prices change, so we re-check them before every workshop and tell you what is current on the day.

Who it is for

Good fit

  • Final-year engineering students (ECE, EEE, CS, instrumentation)
  • Engineers in embedded, software or test who are curious about silicon
  • Anyone who has written a little code and knows what a flip-flop is

Not required

  • Prior chip-design experience
  • Paid tools or a licence of any kind
  • A powerful laptop: the heavy flow runs in the cloud
