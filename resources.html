What the programs are built on

An open-source toolchain, 100 study guides, labs with automated checks, a quiz bank and slide decks. The course materials themselves are given to enrolled students and partner institutions; this page describes what is in them.

The open-source toolchain

Everything you learn on, you can install at home and keep. No licences, no licence servers.

Foundations

Linux (native or WSL2)
The shell every engineering flow runs in.
Git
Version control, pull requests and code review; the base of your portfolio.
Python 3
Scripting, testbenches, reference models, checkers and plots.
Docker
The pinned lab image, so everyone runs the same tool versions.

Design and verification

cocotb

100 study guides in 8 areas

Short, self-contained guides behind the labs, about two pages each: what it is, a diagram, key ideas, a worked example, common mistakes and "check yourself" questions. Mentors assign them as pre-reading; they double as interview preparation.

15 guides

01 Digital design

Number systems, Boolean algebra, adders, setup and hold, FSMs, synthesizable Verilog, reset strategies, clock domain crossing, FIFOs, pipelining, low-power design.

10 guides

02 Computer architecture and RISC-V

ISA basics, RV32I, single-cycle to pipelined CPUs, hazards and forwarding, branch prediction, caches, virtual memory, interrupts and CSRs, on-chip buses, SoC memory maps.

20 guides

03 Verification

Verification planning, testbench architecture, constrained-random testing, functional and code coverage, cocotb, UVM and pyuvm, scoreboards, assertions, formal, equivalence checking, gate-level simulation, UPF.

15 guides

04 Physical design, timing and test

RTL to GDS, synthesis, STA and SDC, floorplanning, placement, clock trees, routing, physical verification, sign-off, IR drop, DFT and scan, MBIST and JTAG, open PDKs, tape-out and bring-up.

10 guides

05 Devices, analog and process

The MOSFET, the CMOS inverter, fabrication, PVT corners, current mirrors and amplifiers, op-amps and comparators, data converters, PLLs, analog layout, packaging and chiplets.

15 guides

06 Embedded systems

MCU architecture, embedded C, boot and startup, linker scripts, interrupts, UART/SPI/I2C, timers and watchdogs, DMA, RTOS concepts, Zephyr, drivers, low-power firmware, bootloaders, debugging, firmware testing.

5 guides

07 Edge AI and hardware for ML

Neural networks for hardware engineers, quantization, TinyML on microcontrollers, MAC and systolic arrays, hardware/software co-design.

10 guides

08 Systems engineering and professional skills

Requirements and traceability, MBSE, FMEA, product lifecycle and DFM, agile for hardware, estimation and risk, technical writing, design reviews, Git collaboration, using AI tools responsibly.

Labs by track

Each lab is done by hand, then with AI, and checked automatically. Titles only; the labs are given to enrolled students.

Common core

  • Warm-up: shell, Git and Python
  • 8-bit ALU, tested exhaustively
  • Parameterized counter
  • Sequence-detector FSM
  • UART transmitter
  • Synchronous FIFO
  • Single-cycle RV32I core and SoC

Track A · Design and verification

  • Dual-clock FIFO and CDC
  • UART TX/RX coverage closure
  • APB register block with pyuvm
  • Formal proofs of FIFO properties
  • ISA tests with a lockstep reference
  • Timer peripheral: lint, regression, CI

Track B · Physical design

  • Synthesis to sky130 standard cells
  • Floorplan, power grid and placement
  • Clock tree, routing and timing repair
  • Static timing across corners
  • DRC and LVS with seeded errors
  • Tape-out readiness and precheck

Track D · Embedded and edge AI

  • Bare-metal startup and UART
  • Interrupts and a lock-free ring buffer
  • Preemptive scheduling and response-time analysis
  • PCB design review
  • TinyML: int8 inference in C
  • Firmware unit tests, mutation testing, CI

Track E · Systems and product

  • Requirements and traceability
  • Trade study and architecture
  • Power, latency, mass and cost budgets
  • FMEA
  • BOM costing and test strategy
  • V&V plan and product review

Also included

  • 8 bug-hunt exercises: find, prove, fix
  • 4 capstone kits with data sets and reference models
  • Advanced electives: gate-level simulation, portable stimulus, UPF
  • Templates: spec, test plan, review checklist, AI log, status, postmortem
  • Interview question bank

Track C (analog and mixed-signal) labs come with the track, in a later cohort.

Quiz bank and slide decks

400-question quiz bank

Four auto-graded questions per guide, built from its "check yourself" questions, core ideas and worked example. Weekly quizzes per track, taken in the terminal or on paper. Every wrong answer comes back with a one-line explanation. Single-choice, select-all and numeric questions.

Slide decks

One deck per teaching day, written as text so they live next to the labs and guides they teach, with detail kept in speaker notes. The twelve common-core days are ready; track decks follow.

Getting the materials. The content is not published for download. Enrolled students receive everything for their program, and partner institutions receive what their program uses. To ask about access for your college or company, use the partner form.
