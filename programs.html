Programs

The 12-week flagship, silicon to product

A full-time, mentored program: two weeks of common core, six weeks in one specialization track, and four weeks building a product in a mixed-track team. Every tool is open source. First cohort planned for 2027.

Weeks 3–8 · Specialization tracks

Pick one track. Each has a lab every week, weekly design reviews and written specs. Every track follows the same shape: outcomes, week plan, tools, assessment.

Weeks 9–12 · Team capstone

Teams of four, mixed across tracks, build one small product end to end: a custom RTL block, verified, running on an FPGA with firmware, and demonstrated on demo day. Every project can also run in simulation only, on recorded sensor data, so no team is blocked by hardware.

Planned: future capstone problems will be drawn from Ketuna's India-relevant product projects (drone control, NavIC, smart metering, battery management and more), which are work in progress today.

P1 · ★★

Vibration monitor

Motors and pumps fail after weeks of rising vibration. Build a streaming RMS, peak and threshold engine for a 3-axis accelerometer, and firmware that logs features and raises an alert after several windows over the threshold.

Demo: shake the board, or replay a "failing pump" recording, and the alert fires.

P2 · ★★★

Keyword-spotting badge

Recognise one spoken keyword on a battery badge with no cloud. Build an 8-bit multiply-accumulate array that speeds up a tiny network's dense layers, bit-exact with an int8 reference, plus firmware that runs the rest.

Demo: play a clip and the LED lights; report cycles per inference against firmware only.

P3 · ★★

Soil-sensor node

Fields have no power outlet. Build an SPI sensor interface with oversampling and a CRC-8 packet framer, and low-power firmware with a battery budget that has to last a season.

Demo: live readings, a corrupted packet rejected, the battery estimate defended.

P4 · ★★

Gesture controller

Turn wrist flicks and twists into commands, such as a presentation clicker. Build IMU pre-processing (moving average, rate of change, debounced events) and firmware that classifies gestures.

Demo: live gestures, or a replay, move slides forward and back; accuracy report.

The capstone and the review and spec work together outweigh the labs: the program grades engineering judgment, not just finished code.

How labs and reviews work

  1. By hand

    Do the lab yourself, from the spec.

  2. Ask the AI

    Do it again with an AI assistant.

  3. Review first

    Read its output before you run it.

  4. Prove it

    Tests, lint, formal or a reference model.

  5. Log it

    An AI log: what was wrong and how you found out.

Checked the same way, every time

RTL labs are checked by simulation with a directed testbench, a cocotb test and lint; non-RTL labs have a self-check that prints pass or a clear reason. The same regression runs in CI on every push, so "it works on my machine" is never the end of the story.

Reviews that teach

Every week ends in a design review against a checklist, with recorded actions you close the next week. Submitting AI output without checking it counts as a professional-conduct issue, not only a lost mark.

Admissions

The test checks fundamentals and whether you can write working code under time pressure, not prior VLSI experience. Applications open with the first cohort's dates.

  1. Apply

    A short form: degree, year, why this program, and your code profile if you have one. Aimed at B.E., B.Tech or M.Tech in ECE, EEE or CS, final year or recent graduates.

  2. 60-minute test

    Online, 100 marks. AI tools, internet search and help from others are not allowed: admission measures what you can do alone.

  3. 20-minute call

    Walk us through your own Verilog answer, your motivation, and your plan to commit 12 weeks full-time.

Test format

SectionWhat it checksTimeMarks
A · Digital logic10 multiple-choice questions: gates, number systems, counters, timing basics15 min20
B · Programming5 short answers in C and Python, each with one line of reasoning15 min20
C · VerilogOne small combinational module, auto-checked on every input combination, plus marks for code quality (readable, no latches)20 min40
D · PythonOne short function, auto-checked against hidden test cases10 min20

Sample style, not a real question: "How many flip-flops does a counter that counts 0 to 11 need? Give the answer and one line of reasoning." Verilog-2001 or SystemVerilog and Python 3.8+ are accepted. Everyone in a batch gets the same paper and time limit; retakes use a different paper.

Lab platform and toolchain

The planned approach is laptops first, with a cloud fallback: everyone runs the same pinned lab image on their own laptop, and a few pre-built cloud machines from the same image cover laptops that fail the week-0 check and heavy physical-design runs. You leave with a working open-source setup on your own machine.

Week 0

  • An environment check script and the lab image on your laptop
  • An install clinic for anyone who gets stuck
  • Same day fallback to a cloud machine if your laptop cannot run it
  • Board weeks (embedded, capstone) run over USB on your laptop or a lab bench PC

Laptop guide To be confirmed

About: 64-bit, 4 cores, 8 GB RAM (16 GB for physical design), 40 GB free SSD, virtualisation enabled, a recent Windows with WSL2 or a recent Linux. macOS works through containers, with a cloud fallback for the heaviest runs. The final spec is published with the admission pack.
